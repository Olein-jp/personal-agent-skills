# Drafting Checklist

## Purpose and Reader

- Does the chapter deliver the outcome defined by the outline?
- Does it assume only the knowledge granted by the reader profile and earlier chapters?
- Does each section earn its place in the chapter?
- Does the draft meet the requested approximate length without padding or unsupported claims?

## Evidence

- Can every concrete technical claim be traced to a supplied source or clearly identified observation?
- Are versions, dates, prices, UI labels, URLs, and compatibility statements treated as time-sensitive?
- Are unresolved claims marked or reported instead of guessed?

## Structure

- Does the explanation move from context to concept to action to verification?
- Are steps in executable order?
- Are examples introduced before details that depend on them?
- Are transitions with adjacent chapters clear without repeating their content?

## Manuscript Quality

- Does terminology match the repository's canonical forms?
- Does Markdown comply with the publishing rules?
- Are notes, drafting commentary, and source-management details kept outside publishable prose?
- Has the draft been revised once after reading it as a reader?
